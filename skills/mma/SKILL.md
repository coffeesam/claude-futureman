# MMA: Mindshare, Marketshare, Awareness

Measure the brand on three separate things, find the weakest, and say what would move it. The three often move independently, and treating them as one hides the real problem.

## Definitions

These are Sam Hon's definitions. They differ from common MBA usage, where "mindshare" often means being top of mind. **Always open the analysis with this line** so the reader uses the same meanings:

> *MMA, as used here: Awareness = people know or think of the brand. Marketshare = paying or revenue-driving actions, counted. Mindshare = loyalty, from trust up to preorder.*

**Awareness.** Potential customers know the brand, or think of it when the need comes up. Evidence: reach, followers, search volume, branded search, aided and unaided recall, press.

**Marketshare.** Someone pays, or takes an action that leads to revenue. Example: Google Search is free to use, but advertisers pay for the clicks and conversions it drives. Always counted, though not always as a percentage. Use whichever fits: revenue, paying users, transactions, or countable records such as orders, invoices, bookings, tickets or deliverables.

**Mindshare.** Loyalty to the brand. It is a ladder. Place the brand on the highest rung it reliably reaches, from top to bottom:

1. **Preorder:** customers pay before the product is ready to ship
2. **Ambassadorship:** customers champion the brand unprompted
3. **Referral:** customers recommend it when asked
4. **Shared values:** customers identify with the company's mission or vision
5. **Trust:** customers trust the company

Evidence: churn and repeat purchase, referral share of new customers, reviews and community, waitlists and preorders.

## Common patterns

- **High Awareness, low Marketshare:** people know it but do not buy. A conversion, pricing or product-fit problem, not a reach problem.
- **High Marketshare, low Mindshare:** people buy but would switch. Exposed to any cheaper or better competitor.
- **High Mindshare, low Awareness:** loved by a few. The next move is reach, and the loyal customers are the best channel for it.

## Method

1. Say what is being measured and against which market or competitor.
2. For each of the three, give the evidence in the user's own numbers. If a number is missing, say which one would settle it. Do not invent figures.
3. Place Mindshare on the ladder.
4. Name the weakest of the three, and the one move most likely to lift it.

## Output

Open with the definitions line above.

Then one sentence that answers the question: which measure is weakest, and what to do about it.

Then **Awareness**, **Marketshare** and **Mindshare**, two to four lines each, with the evidence. For Mindshare, name the rung.

End with **Next move**: one concrete action, and the number that would show it is working.

Keep it under about 15 lines. This skill runs straight away; it does not need to offer first.
