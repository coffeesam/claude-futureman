# SPINOVA

Understand the other side's situation before deciding what to offer them, then check honestly whether the user can deliver. The first four steps are about them. The last three are about the user.

## First: run now, or offer?

The full assessment is long. Do not make the user wait for it unasked.

**Run it now** if any of these is true:
- The user's message starts with `/futureman:spinova`.
- The user asks for a SPINOVA analysis, or a full opportunity assessment, in their own words.
- The user said yes to your offer to run it.

**Otherwise, offer.** In two to four sentences, give your quick take: whether the opportunity looks worth pursuing, and the one angle to lead with. Then add one line offering the full assessment, for example:

> Want me to run a full SPINOVA assessment, covering their situation, problem and needs, and whether you have the value and ability to win it?

Then stop. Do not start the assessment in the same reply.

## The steps

1. **Situation:** the counterpart's current state. Who they are, what they do now, what has changed.
2. **Problem:** what is wrong or missing for them, in their terms, not the user's.
3. **Implication:** what happens to them if the problem is not solved. Cost, risk, lost time or revenue. Quantify with their numbers where possible.
4. **Need:** what a solution must do for them. Write it as outcomes, not features.
5. **Opportunity:** what solving it opens up, for them and for the user (follow-on work, reference customer, new market).
6. **Value:** what the user specifically adds that others cannot or will not. Be honest: if the value is generic, say so.
7. **Ability:** whether the user can deliver.
   - **Capacity:** people, time and money
   - **Capability:** tools, systems and processes
   - **Competency:** skills and track record

## Output (when running)

Start with the verdict in one sentence: **Pursue** or **Pass**, and the angle to lead with.

Then the seven steps, each in bold with one to three lines. Ability gets one line each for capacity, capability and competency.

End with **Biggest gap**: the weakest step, and how to close it before the pitch, or why it means passing.

## Rules

- Steps 1 to 4 are written from the counterpart's point of view. Do not mention the user's product until Value.
- Use the user's facts. Where a fact is missing, write what to find out in discovery, rather than inventing it.
- A weak Ability is a reason to pass or partner, not something to gloss over.
- Plain words. Technical terms stay exact.
