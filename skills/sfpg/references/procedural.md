# Procedural: empower, delegate, follow up

Procedural principles define how intent becomes accountable execution. In a traditional organisation they are workflows, SOPs, handoffs, reviews, standups and escalation paths. In an AI-native organisation they map to `Workflow.md`.

| Stage | Human meaning | AI-native meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Empower** | Give enough context, authority, resources and boundaries to act | Context provisioning: the objective, relevant context, tools, approved data, permissions, success criteria and constraints |
| **Delegate** | Assign the right work to the right person, team, agent, skill or system | Routing and orchestration: by task type, capability, data access and escalation rules |
| **Follow up** | Make sure the work reached the intended outcome | Monitoring, evaluation and feedback: verify outputs, catch exceptions, retry failed steps, escalate, record what was learned |

## Formulas

- **Empower** = context + tools + authority + boundaries
- **Delegate** = route the right work to the right execution unit
- **Follow up** = monitor, evaluate, escalate, learn, and close the loop

## Typical failures

- **Not empowered:** shallow work. People or agents guess, hallucinate, or ask unnecessary questions, because they do not know what success looks like.
- **Badly delegated:** everything goes to one generic person or assistant, which becomes the bottleneck.
- **No follow-up:** work is assigned, but nobody checks it was correct, complete, timely or useful. This is where execution most often breaks down. Without follow-up, AI creates outputs. With it, AI creates accountable execution.

## Writing empowerment rules

For each role or agent, write four lists: **must receive**, **may do**, **may not do**, and **success criteria**.
